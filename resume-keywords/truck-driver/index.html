Common mistakes on Truck Driver resumes

  • Omitting specific license classes or endorsements.
  • Listing general driving experience without mentioning DOT compliance.
  • Ignoring the mention of safety records or accident-free miles.
  • Using vague terms like good driver instead of technical skills.

FAQ

Which CDL keywords are most important?

Include your specific license class, such as Class A or B, and any endorsements like Hazmat or Tanker. These terms tell recruiters exactly what equipment you can legally operate.

How do I show safety on a resume?

List your total accident-free miles or years of clean driving. Mention your adherence to DOT safety regulations and pre-trip inspection habits.

Should I list ELD experience?

Yes, list the specific Electronic Logging Device software you use. This proves you can manage Hours of Service logs without manual errors.

Do endorsements matter for local driving?

Endorsements increase your value even for local roles. Tanker or Hazmat certifications allow you to handle specialized freight that pays more.

How do I describe route planning?

Mention your use of GPS tools and coordination with dispatchers. Focus on how you reduce idle time and meet delivery windows.

What is a TWIC card and should I list it?

A Transportation Worker Identification Credential allows access to secure ports. List it if you apply for port-related or maritime hauling jobs.
