Common mistakes on HVAC Technician resumes

  • Listing only 'HVAC experience' without specifying equipment types (RTU, chiller, VRF, residential split)
  • Omitting EPA 608 certification type (Type I, II, III, or Universal) and expiration date
  • Failing to mention diagnostic tools used daily (manifold gauges, combustion analyzer, megohmmeter)
  • Writing generic duties instead of outcomes like reduced callbacks, improved efficiency, or warranty compliance

FAQ

What are the most important HVAC Technician resume keywords for ATS?

The most important keywords are EPA 608 Universal Certification, specific equipment types like RTU, chiller, VRF, and mini-split, and diagnostic tools such as manifold gauges, multimeters, and combustion analyzers. Include control protocols like BACnet or Modbus if you have BAS experience.

Should I list EPA 608 certification on my HVAC resume?

Yes, always list your EPA 608 certification type (Universal, Type I, II, or III) and the certification number or expiration date. It is a legal requirement for handling refrigerants and the first filter recruiters and ATS apply.

How do I show commercial vs residential experience on an HVAC resume?

Create separate sections or bullet groups for Commercial and Residential. Under each, name the specific equipment you serviced (e.g., 'Carrier 50-ton RTUs' or 'Daikin VRV systems'), the scope (tonnage, square footage), and the control systems involved.

What technical skills do HVAC hiring managers look for beyond repair?

Hiring managers value preventive maintenance planning using CMMS, startup and commissioning procedures, load calculations (Manual J/D), air balancing with hoods, and integration with Building Automation Systems. Brazing, blueprint reading, and electrical schematics are also expected.

How should I format an HVAC resume for applicant tracking systems?

Use a clean, single-column layout with standard headings like Certifications, Technical Skills, and Work History. Place EPA 608 and NATE certifications near the top. Embed keywords from the job description naturally in your bullet points. Avoid graphics, columns, or tables that parsers cannot read.

Do I need manufacturer-specific training on my HVAC resume?

Yes, list factory-authorized training for major brands like Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Daikin, or Mitsubishi. It proves you can perform warranty work and handle proprietary controls or diagnostic software, which is a strong differentiator for commercial and VRF roles.
