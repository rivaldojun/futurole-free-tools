Common mistakes on Physical Therapist resumes

  • Listing generic duties like 'treated patients' without specifying the condition or outcome.
  • Omitting specific certification details or license numbers required for verification.
  • Using outdated terminology instead of current clinical standards like ICF or ICD-10.
  • Failing to quantify patient volume or complexity, which hides the scope of clinical experience.

FAQ

What are the most important Physical Therapist resume keywords for ATS?

Include specific clinical areas like orthopedics or neurology, along with technical terms such as manual therapy and gait analysis. These terms match job descriptions and trigger ATS filters effectively.

Should I list my DPT degree or PT degree first?

List your highest degree first. If you hold a Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT), place it above a Master's or Bachelor's degree to highlight your advanced clinical training.

How do I show patient outcomes without violating HIPAA?

Use aggregate data or percentages rather than specific patient details. Phrases like 'improved mobility scores by 20%' demonstrate impact while maintaining patient privacy.

Do I need to list every certification on my resume?

List only certifications relevant to the specific job. Include board certifications like SCS if they match the role's focus, but omit unrelated credentials to save space.

What is the best format for a Physical Therapist resume?

Use a reverse-chronological format. This structure allows recruiters to see your most recent clinical experience and education first, which is standard for healthcare roles.

How should I describe my experience with electronic health records?

Name specific systems like Epic or Cerner in your skills section. Mentioning these tools shows you can integrate seamlessly into existing hospital or clinic workflows.
