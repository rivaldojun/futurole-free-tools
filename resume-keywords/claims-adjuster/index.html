Resume bullet examples for a Claims Adjuster

  • Investigated [X] claims across [X] lines of business, reviewed coverage, and documented findings for accurate resolution.
  • Negotiated settlements with claimants and attorneys, reaching agreed resolutions while keeping claim files complete.
  • Reviewed [X] damage estimates, verified covered losses, and issued coverage decisions with supporting documentation.
  • Identified claim patterns that met referral criteria and referred suspected fraud to [X] for further review.
  • Processed first notice of loss, collected recorded statements, and maintained accurate case notes in [X] claims system.
  • Recovered [X] through subrogation activities and coordinated with [X] to close out recovery files.

Common mistakes on Claims Adjuster resumes

  • Listing broad duties without naming the claims tasks, such as handling customer service calls without mentioning investigation, coverage review, or settlement.
  • Using employer-specific acronyms and internal system names that an ATS or recruiter outside the company cannot interpret.
  • Leaving out the insurance line of business, since auto, property, workers' compensation, and liability claims use different terminology.
  • Writing adjustment results without a clear action, such as saying reviewed claims without showing what you decided, documented, or resolved.

FAQ

What are the best keywords for a claims adjuster resume?

Use terms that match the claims workflow: claims investigation, policy coverage review, liability determination, damage assessment, settlement negotiation, and claims documentation. Add the insurance line you handled, such as auto, property, workers' compensation, or general liability, plus the systems and tools you used.

How do I write claims adjuster resume bullets that show results?

Name the task, the action you took, and the outcome, such as investigating claims, reviewing coverage, and resolving cases within a service standard. Replace the bracketed fields with your real caseload, claim types, settlement figures, or recovery amounts.

Which claims adjuster skills should I put on my resume?

Show the full claims process, from first notice of loss and recorded statements through coverage decisions, damage review, and settlement negotiation. Include supporting skills such as claims documentation, fraud referral, subrogation, case management, and communication with claimants, attorneys, and other insurers.

Do I need a license or certification to work as a claims adjuster?

Requirements depend on your state and the insurance line you handle. Many adjusters work under a state licensing requirement, and some employers ask for a designation such as the Certified Insurance Adjuster (CIE). Check the posting and your state rules before listing credentials on your resume.

Should I list claims management software on my resume?

List the software only if you used it, and pair the product name with the task it supported. For example, write that you managed case notes and claim status in [X] claims system, so the entry shows both the tool and your claims work.

How do I tailor a claims adjuster resume to a job posting?

Mirror the posting's terminology for claim types, coverage review, settlement authority, and reporting duties. Keep each bullet tied to your own experience, and place the matching skills where the employer can find them without searching through unrelated sections.
