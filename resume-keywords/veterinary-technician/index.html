Resume bullet examples for a Veterinary Technician

  • Administer medications to [X] animals per shift, documenting each dose in the medical record to support safe treatment plans.
  • Perform venipuncture, fecal exams, urinalysis, and other diagnostic tests, delivering results to veterinarians for treatment decisions.
  • Restrain and monitor animals during examinations, vaccinations, and diagnostic procedures, reducing patient stress and improving staff safety.
  • Prepare the surgical room, maintain the sterile field, and assist with anesthesia monitoring and recovery, supporting smooth surgical cases.
  • Educate pet owners on medication, home care, and follow-up visits, improving client understanding and treatment compliance.
  • Clean and disinfect exam rooms, kennels, and equipment, maintaining biosecurity standards and supporting daily clinic operations.

Common mistakes on Veterinary Technician resumes

  • List job duties without outcomes. Add the task, the tool or method, and what changed for the patient, client, or clinic.
  • Use animal care terms without naming your actual tasks. A posting for venipuncture needs the exact phrase if you perform it.
  • Include every certification or skill you have ever studied. Keep the list to what you can discuss and demonstrate.
  • Write a generic objective. Replace it with a short summary naming your role, experience level, clinic setting, and strongest skills.

FAQ

What are the most important veterinary technician resume keywords?

The strongest keywords name the hands-on work you do: animal restraint, medication administration, venipuncture, laboratory testing, surgical support, anesthesia monitoring, radiography, and client education. Add the tools and methods you use, such as digital radiography, fecal analysis, or practice management software. Show each keyword in a bullet with a clear action and result.

How do I get my veterinary technician resume through an ATS?

Use a standard layout with a single-column structure, clear section headings, and plain text that an applicant tracking system can read. Mirror the exact wording from the job posting when it matches your experience, and place those terms in your summary, skills section, and work history. Keep every claim accurate and supported by your duties.

Should I include veterinary technician certifications on my resume?

List certifications that you hold and that relate to the job, including the issuing organization and the year you earned each one. If you have completed coursework but hold no certification yet, list it under training or education. Do not list a credential you have not earned or cannot verify.

How do I write veterinary technician resume bullets without inventing numbers?

Describe the scope of your work using your actual duties, the tools you used, and the outcome you observed. Replace placeholders such as [X] with real details from your records or your supervisor's feedback. If you cannot verify a number, describe the task and its purpose instead of estimating a figure.

What skills should I highlight for a veterinary technician job in a clinic?

Clinic roles often call for restraint, medication administration, diagnostic testing, surgical and dental support, anesthesia monitoring, and client communication. Show the full patient workflow, from check-in and examination through treatment, discharge, and recordkeeping. Name the equipment and software you used so a recruiter can match your experience to the posting.

How do I tailor a veterinary technician resume to a specific job posting?

Read the posting and list the requirements you already meet, then rewrite your summary and bullets to use the same language for those requirements. Address gaps honestly in your cover letter or during the interview.
