Common mistakes on Dietitian resumes

  • Listing generic duties like 'provided nutritional advice' without specifying the clinical context or outcome
  • Omitting the specific credential (RD or RDN) in the header, which is a non-negotiable requirement for most roles
  • Using vague terms like 'helped patients' instead of quantifiable actions like 'assessed' or 'developed'
  • Failing to mention specific software or EHR systems used in clinical or food service settings

FAQ

Do I need to list my RD credential in the resume header?

Yes, place your Registered Dietitian (RD) or Registered Dietitian Nutritionist (RDN) credential directly after your name. This is the primary filter for hiring managers and ATS systems in this field.

What is the difference between RD and RDN on a resume?

RD and RDN are interchangeable titles for the same credential. Use whichever your state or country prefers, but ensure it is clearly visible. Both indicate you have completed the accredited program and passed the national exam.

Should I include nutrition internships if I am a new graduate?

Yes, list your supervised practice hours and internship sites prominently. Highlight specific clinical rotations and any specialized populations you worked with, such as pediatrics or oncology.

How do I quantify results in a clinical dietetics resume?

Use metrics like the number of patients seen, improvement in specific health markers, or reduction in readmission rates. If exact numbers are confidential, use ranges or percentages of improvement.

Is it necessary to list software skills for a clinical dietitian role?

Yes, include specific dietetics software and EHR systems you have used. Familiarity with tools like MedTrainer or Epic demonstrates technical proficiency that reduces onboarding time.

How should I format my skills section for ATS compatibility?

Use a simple, single-column layout with standard headings like 'Skills' or 'Core Competencies'. List hard skills in bullet points using the exact terminology from the job description to ensure keyword matching.
